# Devices: the positive repertoire

Read with `originality.md`, at layout and composition time, when ORIGINALITY is `signature`
or `benchmark`.

`originality.md` says what not to do. A blacklist alone lowers the floor without raising the
ceiling: a rejected default leaves a gap, and the gap gets filled by the next-nearest
default. This file is the vocabulary to fill it with — named devices, each with the
condition it needs, what it breaks on, and what it costs.

**How to use it, and the one way to use it wrong.**

Right: the provenance line in `originality.md` produces a fact about the subject; the fact
suggests a device; the device is named here so it can be built precisely instead of
approximated. Wrong: browsing this list for something that looks good. A device picked from
a list is a default with a longer name, and the swap test will catch it.

Every device below is *neutral* — none of them is more original than another. What makes a
page unmistakable is the fit between the device and the fact, and the discipline of using
one or two rather than six.

> **This file ages.** It describes a repertoire in use around 2026. A device becomes a
> default when enough people use it, and this list will contain defaults within a year or
> two. Re-cut it against real work; do not trust it past its date.

## Composition

**Asymmetric column pair.** Two columns at an uneven ratio — 7/5, 8/4, 9/3 — held for the
whole page rather than alternating. Needs: content with a genuine primary and secondary
(argument and evidence, text and specimen). Breaks: when both columns carry equal weight,
which reads as a mistake. Cost: 0 KB. Reads as editorial confidence; the alternating
image/text zigzag is the default it replaces.

**Anchored spine.** One vertical line — a rule, a column edge, a sticky index, a timeline —
that every section aligns to and one element crosses deliberately. Needs: a sequence or a
hierarchy that the spine is *about*. Breaks: when the spine is decorative, at which point it
is a divider with ambition. Cost: 0 KB.

**Full-bleed against a held measure.** Body text pinned to a strict 60–75 character measure,
with specific elements — a specimen, a table, a figure — breaking to the full viewport. The
contrast between the two widths does the work. Needs: something that genuinely benefits from
width. Breaks: when everything bleeds, which is just a wide page.

**The oversized index.** A table of contents, a specification list, a changelog or a
directory treated as the composition rather than as navigation. Needs: a subject whose value
*is* its catalog — reference material, a component library, an archive, a product with
many named parts. Cost: 0 KB, and it is one of the strongest and least-used answers for
technical subjects.

**Baseline grid, visibly held.** A real baseline rhythm where every element — headings,
figures, captions, rules — lands on the same interval, and it is perceptible. Needs:
discipline and long-form content. Breaks: with mixed embedded media that cannot be
constrained. Cost: 0 KB, high effort, and it is the difference between "clean" and
"typeset".

**One deliberate collision.** Two elements overlapping on purpose — type over a specimen, a
figure crossing a section boundary, a card breaking its container. Needs: everything else on
the page to be strictly aligned, or it reads as a bug. Budget: this is a loud element; spend
it once. `modes.md`.

**Density inversion.** A dense, small, information-rich block adjacent to a near-empty one.
Needs: real information in the dense half. Breaks: when the density is fake — a wall of
lorem-shaped text is the tell. Strong for data, specification, and reference subjects.

**The register shift.** One section that changes its layout language entirely — different
grid, different type treatment, different ground — because the *kind* of content changed.
Needs: a genuine change of kind (argument → reference, marketing → documentation). Breaks:
when used for variety, which is how a page becomes a set of templates stapled together.

## Type

**Two-face contrast with a reason.** The pairing is not display+neutral by reflex; the two
faces come from two different facts about the subject — the material and its notation, the
craft and its paperwork, the instrument and its manual. Needs: both facts to exist. Breaks:
when the second face is decoration, at which point one face was enough.

**One face, many registers.** A single family carrying the whole page through optical size,
weight, width, tracking and case. Needs: a family with real range (a variable font with
optical sizing). Cost: often *less* than two faces. This is the most under-used answer in
the repertoire and it reads as deliberate rather than as economical.

**Type as the subject's own artifact.** A face that is literally the notation of the field —
a monospace where the subject is code, a grotesque where the subject is signage, a face with
real historical provenance where the subject has one. Needs: the provenance fact to be true;
a "technical-looking" mono on a non-technical subject is costume.

**Optical scale, not size steps.** Display type set with tracking, line height and optical
size tuned per role rather than by scaling one setting. Needs: attention at the specific
sizes shipped. Cost: 0 KB. It is most of the perceived difference between typography and
"big text".

**Set numbers as numbers.** Tabular figures in tables, oldstyle in running text, real
fractions, real small caps where the family has them, correct minus and multiplication
signs. Needs: a family with the features. Cost: 0 KB. Invisible when present, immediately
cheap-looking when absent.

**The very long line, held once.** A single line of type at a measure and size that would be
wrong anywhere else — a claim, an epigraph, a definition — with everything around it strictly
measured. Needs: a sentence worth it. Exposes weak copy instantly.

## Color

**Two colors and a ground.** Not a palette — a ground, an ink, and one accent that means
"act". Everything else is a lightness step of those. Needs: the discipline to keep support
colors out. This is what most "premium" pages actually are underneath.

**The ground is not white.** A tinted ground derived from the subject's material — paper,
stone, ink-on-newsprint, a screen phosphor, a fabric — with contrast ratios recomputed
against it, not inherited from a white-ground design. Breaks: when the tint is chosen for
warmth rather than from a fact. Cost: 0 KB.

**Accent by frequency, not by area.** The accent appears in a few square centimeters of the
whole page and is therefore unmissable. Needs: everything actionable to be genuinely rare.
Breaks: the moment three things are accent-colored. `craft.md`.

**Semantic-only color.** Color used exclusively to encode meaning — state, category,
provenance, magnitude — and never decoratively. Needs: a subject with real categories.
Strongest on `product-surface`, where it is often the only distinctiveness available.

**Ink weight instead of hue.** Hierarchy carried entirely by lightness and weight on one
hue. Needs: careful contrast work at every step, since the usual escape hatch (change the
hue) is gone. Cost: 0 KB, and it survives `forced-colors` better than most alternatives.

**Material-derived dark.** A dark ground that is a specific dark — the blue-black of a
photographic base, the brown-black of print ink, the green-black of a phosphor — rather than
neutral near-black with an acid accent, which is banned default #2. Needs: the fact.

## Surface and material

**The rule as structure.** Hairline rules doing real structural work: separating registers,
marking a spine, closing a section. Needs: a consistent rule weight and color token, and
restraint. Breaks: when rules replace spacing, which produces the broadsheet default.

**Real elevation, or none.** Either layered shadows that model a light source consistently
across the whole page, or no shadows at all with structure from ground contrast and rules.
Needs: a decision. The default failure is one 24 px blur applied to everything.

**Ground as depth.** Depth from stacked grounds — a section that sits on a slightly
different ground, an inset panel, a recessed field — rather than from shadow. Cost: 0 KB,
survives dark mode and forced colors intact.

**One material, honestly rendered.** If the subject has a physical material, one place on
the page renders it properly — a real photograph, a real texture at real scale, a real
specimen — instead of everything getting a light gloss. Needs: the asset to exist and to be
worth its bytes. `../quality/engineering.md`.

**Masked edge instead of a border.** `mask-image` gradients to fade a scroll region, a
figure or an overflowing table, so the boundary is a soft fact rather than a hard line.
Cost: 0 KB, widely supported, and it replaces the "fade overlay div" hack.

## Detail

**The caption as a first-class element.** Figures, tables, code and specimens get real
captions, styled deliberately, carrying real information. Needs: something worth captioning.
Cost: 0 KB. It is the single cheapest signal that a page was edited rather than assembled.

**Live state as decoration's replacement.** The one moving thing on the page is a real value
changing — a status, a queue depth, a build time, a count that is actually counted. Needs:
the value to be real; a fake counter is a fabricated metric and is banned outright.
`spectacle.md` Gate A.

**Hover that reveals rather than lifts.** Hover exposes information — the full value, the
source, the definition, the alternative — instead of translating the element upward. Needs:
a non-hover path for touch. `../quality/floor.md`.

**Focus as part of the design.** The focus ring designed with the palette, at ≥3:1 against
both the element and its surroundings, consistent across every control. Needs: nothing. It
is free, it is required anyway, and a designed focus ring is one of the few details that
reads as care to the people most likely to notice.

**The empty state as the best screen.** The state a person sees first, treated as the most
designed screen in the product rather than as an apology. Needs: a real first action to
offer. Strongest device available on `product-surface` at `quiet` budget.

**Correct typographic punctuation.** Real quotation marks, en and em dashes used correctly,
non-breaking spaces where a number and its unit must not split, `&shy;` where a long term
must break. Cost: 0 KB. Its absence is a tell; its presence is invisible, which is the point.

## Choosing

1. Take the provenance fact. Ask what the fact *implies* structurally — a sequence implies a
   spine, a catalog implies an index, a material implies a ground.
2. Name **one** composition device and **one** type device. Everything else stays
   conventional. Two devices held perfectly beat six devices approximated.
3. Check the pair against the budget in `modes.md`. A device that breaks the page's own
   rules is a loud element and is counted.
4. Run the swap test per axis. A device that would fit any subject in the category was
   picked from a list rather than derived from a fact — go back to step 1.
