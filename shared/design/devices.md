# Devices: the positive repertoire

Read with `originality.md`, at layout and composition time, when ORIGINALITY is `signature`
or `benchmark`.

`originality.md` says what not to do. A blacklist alone lowers the floor without raising the
ceiling: a rejected default leaves a gap, and the gap gets filled by the next-nearest
default. This file is the vocabulary to fill it with.

**Enter through the fact, never through the list.** A device browsed for and liked is a
default with a longer name, and the swap test will catch it. The route is: provenance fact →
what the fact implies structurally → the device that does that → build it precisely. The
index below exists so the second arrow is short.

No device here is more original than another. What makes a page unmistakable is the fit
between the device and the fact, and the discipline of holding one or two rather than six.

> **This file ages.** It describes a repertoire in use around 2026. A device becomes a
> default when enough people use it. Re-cut it against real work; do not trust it past its
> date.

## The index: what a fact implies

Find the row your provenance fact actually belongs to. If two rows fit, the fact is not yet
specific enough — go back to `originality.md` rather than picking the more interesting row.

| The fact names… | Structurally that implies | Reach for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A sequence, a process, fixed stages, a schedule | A line everything hangs off | **Anchored spine** · **Set numbers as numbers** |
| A catalog, an inventory, many named parts, an archive | The list *is* the value | **The oversized index** · **The caption as a first-class element** |
| A material, a substrate, a physical object | Ground and surface | **The ground is not white** · **One material, honestly rendered** · **Ground as depth** |
| A notation, a script, a way the field writes | Type is the subject | **Type as the subject's own artifact** · **Set numbers as numbers** |
| A measurement, a reading, live data, a quantity | Density, and one number that moves | **Density inversion** · **Live state as decoration's replacement** |
| An argument with evidence, a claim and its proof | A primary and a secondary that never swap | **Asymmetric column pair** · **The very long line, held once** |
| A specimen, an instrument, a thing worth looking at | A held measure that one element escapes | **Full-bleed against a held measure** · **One deliberate collision** |
| A boundary, a state, a category, a provenance | Color that encodes rather than decorates | **Semantic-only color** · **Accent by frequency, not by area** |
| A dark environment — night work, a screen, a darkroom | A specific dark, not near-black | **Material-derived dark** · **Ink weight instead of hue** |
| Reading, record, reference, long-form authority | Rhythm you can feel | **Baseline grid, visibly held** · **Optical scale, not size steps** |

## Composition

**Asymmetric column pair.** Two columns at an uneven ratio — 7/5, 8/4, 9/3 — held for the
whole page rather than alternating. Needs: content with a genuine primary and secondary.
Breaks: when both columns carry equal weight, which reads as a mistake. Cost: 0 KB. It is
what replaces the alternating image/text zigzag.

**Anchored spine.** One vertical line — a rule, a column edge, a sticky index, a timeline —
that every section aligns to and one element crosses deliberately. Needs: a sequence or a
hierarchy the spine is *about*. Breaks: when the spine is decorative, at which point it is a
divider with ambition. Cost: 0 KB.

**Full-bleed against a held measure.** Body text pinned to a strict 60–75 character measure,
with specific elements — a specimen, a table, a figure — breaking to the full viewport. The
contrast between the two widths does the work. Needs: something that genuinely benefits from
width. Breaks: when everything bleeds, which is just a wide page.

**The oversized index.** A table of contents, a specification list, a changelog or a
directory treated as the composition rather than as navigation. Needs: a subject whose value
*is* its catalog. Cost: 0 KB, and it is the strongest and least-used answer for technical
subjects.

**Baseline grid, visibly held.** A real baseline rhythm where every element — headings,
figures, captions, rules — lands on the same interval, and it is perceptible. Needs:
discipline and long-form content. Breaks: with mixed embedded media that cannot be
constrained. Cost: 0 KB, high effort, and it is the difference between "clean" and "typeset".

**Density inversion.** A dense, small, information-rich block adjacent to a near-empty one.
Needs: real information in the dense half — a wall of lorem-shaped text is the tell. Strong
for data, specification and reference subjects.

**The register shift.** One section that changes its layout language entirely — different
grid, different type treatment, different ground — because the *kind* of content changed.
Needs: a genuine change of kind. Breaks: when used for variety, which is how a page becomes
a set of templates stapled together.

**One deliberate collision.** Two elements overlapping on purpose — type over a specimen, a
figure crossing a section boundary, a card breaking its container. Needs: everything else
strictly aligned, or it reads as a bug. This is a loud element; spend it once. `modes.md`.

## Type

**Type as the subject's own artifact.** A face that is literally the notation of the field —
a monospace where the subject is code, a grotesque where the subject is signage, a face with
real historical provenance where the subject has one. Needs: the provenance fact to be true;
a "technical-looking" mono on a non-technical subject is costume.

**One face, many registers.** A single family carrying the whole page through optical size,
weight, width, tracking and case. Needs: a family with real range. Cost: often *less* than
two faces. The most under-used answer in the repertoire, and it reads as deliberate rather
than as economical. It is also the right answer whenever the subject yields only one fact —
two faces need two facts. `craft.md`, deriving the typeface.

**Optical scale, not size steps.** Display type set with tracking, line height and optical
size tuned per role rather than by scaling one setting. Cost: 0 KB. It is most of the
perceived difference between typography and "big text".

**Set numbers as numbers.** Tabular figures in tables, oldstyle in running text, real
fractions, real small caps where the family has them, correct minus and multiplication signs.
Needs: a family with the features. Cost: 0 KB. Invisible when present, immediately
cheap-looking when absent.

**The very long line, held once.** A single line of type at a measure and size that would be
wrong anywhere else — a claim, an epigraph, a definition — with everything around it strictly
measured. Needs: a sentence worth it. Exposes weak copy instantly.

## Color and material

**Two colors and a ground.** Not a palette — a ground, an ink, and one accent that means
"act". Everything else is a lightness step of those. Needs: the discipline to keep support
colors out. This is what most "premium" pages actually are underneath.

**The ground is not white.** A tinted ground derived from the subject's material, with
contrast ratios recomputed against it rather than inherited from a white-ground design.
Breaks: when the tint is chosen for warmth rather than from a fact. Cost: 0 KB.

**Material-derived dark.** A dark ground that is a *specific* dark — the blue-black of a
photographic base, the brown-black of print ink, the green-black of a phosphor — rather than
neutral near-black with an acid accent, which is banned default #2. Needs: the fact.

**Accent by frequency, not by area.** The accent appears in a few square centimetres of the
whole page and is therefore unmissable. Needs: everything actionable to be genuinely rare.
Breaks: the moment three things are accent-colored. `craft.md`, how much of each color.

**Semantic-only color.** Color used exclusively to encode meaning — state, category,
provenance, magnitude — never decoratively. Needs: a subject with real categories. Strongest
on `product-surface`, where it is often the only distinctiveness available.

**Ink weight instead of hue.** Hierarchy carried entirely by lightness and weight on one hue.
Needs: careful contrast work at every step, since the usual escape hatch is gone. Cost: 0 KB,
and it survives `forced-colors` better than most alternatives.

**Ground as depth.** Depth from stacked grounds — a section on a slightly different ground,
an inset panel, a recessed field — rather than from shadow. Cost: 0 KB, survives dark mode
and forced colors intact. It replaces the one-24px-blur-on-everything default.

**One material, honestly rendered.** If the subject has a physical material, one place on the
page renders it properly — a real photograph, a real texture at real scale, a real specimen —
instead of everything getting a light gloss. Needs: the asset to exist and to be worth its
bytes. **If it does not exist, do not simulate it**: a gradient mesh or a noise overlay
standing in for a material is banned defaults #5 and #6, arrived at by a nicer route. Fall
back to *ground as depth* and an SVG pattern derived from the fact — a real weave, a real
rule spacing, a real notation mark — at 0 KB. `../quality/engineering.md`.

**The rule as structure.** Hairline rules doing real structural work: separating registers,
marking a spine, closing a section. Needs: a consistent rule weight and color token, and
restraint. Breaks: when rules replace spacing, which produces the broadsheet default.

## Detail

**The caption as a first-class element.** Figures, tables, code and specimens get real
captions, styled deliberately, carrying real information. Cost: 0 KB. The single cheapest
signal that a page was edited rather than assembled.

**Live state as decoration's replacement.** The one moving thing on the page is a real value
changing — a status, a queue depth, a build time, a count that is actually counted. Needs:
the value to be real; a fake counter is a fabricated metric and is banned outright.
`spectacle.md` Gate A.

**Hover that reveals rather than lifts.** Hover exposes information — the full value, the
source, the definition — instead of translating the element upward. Needs: a non-hover path
for touch. `../quality/floor.md`.

**Focus as part of the design.** The focus ring designed with the palette, at ≥3:1 against
both the element and its surroundings, consistent across every control. Free, required
anyway, and one of the few details that reads as care to the people most likely to notice.

**The empty state as the best screen.** The state a person sees first, treated as the most
designed screen in the product rather than as an apology. Needs: a real first action to
offer. The strongest device available on `product-surface` at `quiet` budget.

**Correct typographic punctuation.** Real quotation marks, en and em dashes used correctly,
non-breaking spaces where a number and its unit must not split, `&shy;` where a long term
must break. Cost: 0 KB. Its absence is a tell; its presence is invisible, which is the point.

## Choosing

1. Take the provenance fact and find its row in the index. The row is a starting point, not
   a permission slip — a fact that fits no row is usually the most promising one you have.
2. Name **one** composition device and **one** type device. Everything else stays
   conventional. Two devices held perfectly beat six approximated.
3. Check the pair against the budget in `modes.md`. A device that breaks the page's own rules
   is a loud element and is counted.
4. Write the device into the contract's `LAYOUT` provenance line, and dimension it: the
   `PATH` and `DENSITY` lines say what the device does to the reader, and `COLUMNS` says it
   in numbers. A device named but not dimensioned does not survive contact with the CSS, and
   a device with no effect on the density map was decorative. `craft.md`.
5. Run the swap test per axis. A device that would fit any subject in the category was picked
   from a list rather than derived from a fact — go back to step 1.
